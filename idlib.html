Idlib

إدلب

Idlib and its surrounding governorate form a distinctive part of northwestern Syria, known for fertile countryside, olive groves, historic towns, archaeological landscapes, and communities whose traditions and memories are deeply connected to the land.

Olive Country

Olive cultivation has long shaped the landscape and economy of Idlib. Groves, orchards, farms, villages, and local food traditions form an important part of the region’s identity and everyday life.

Ancient Villages

Idlib Governorate contains major parts of the Ancient Villages of Northern Syria, a UNESCO World Heritage property preserving remarkable examples of rural settlements, churches, homes, tombs, water systems, and agricultural landscapes from late antiquity and the Byzantine period.

Towns and Communities

Idlib’s story extends far beyond the city itself. Places such as Ariha, Jisr al-Shughur, Maarrat al-Numan, Sarmada, and many smaller towns and villages contribute to a region with varied local identities, traditions, and histories.

Memory, Displacement, and Renewal

Recent decades brought enormous upheaval, displacement, destruction, and social change to Idlib. Yet the region remains defined by the endurance of its people and by continuing efforts to preserve community life, heritage, education, agriculture, and a sense of home.
