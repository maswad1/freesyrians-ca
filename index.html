OUR MISSION

Freedom, dignity, justice, and a better future for Syria.

Free Syrians is an independent platform dedicated to Syrians who believe in freedom, dignity, justice, and a better future for Syria.

Our mission is to preserve the voices, stories, history, and aspirations of Syrians while creating a space that connects people across generations and around the world.

We believe Syria belongs to all its people. A future Syria should respect human dignity, protect individual rights, value its diversity, and allow every Syrian to participate freely in shaping the country’s future.

Free Syrians aims to inform, document, remember, connect, and contribute to that future.

SYRIA

A country of deep history, diverse communities, and a future still being written.

Syria is more than the conflicts that have defined recent headlines. It is a country shaped by ancient civilizations, historic cities, rich traditions, many communities, and generations of Syrians whose lives and stories extend far beyond politics and war.

This section will grow into a living guide to Syria: its history, cities, culture, people, modern experience, displacement and diaspora, and the ideas shaping its future.

Our goal is to help Syrians and non-Syrians understand the country in its full depth — remembering what was lost, preserving what remains, and looking toward what can still be built.

Explore Syria

Damascus

Syria’s capital and one of the world’s oldest continuously inhabited cities, shaped by centuries of history, faith, trade, architecture, and culture.

Homs

A major city in central Syria with deep historical roots and an important place in the country’s social, cultural, and modern story.

Hama

A historic city on the Orontes River, widely known for its great wooden waterwheels and a distinctive cultural heritage.

Aleppo

An ancient northern city shaped by trade, diverse communities, historic markets, and the monumental Citadel at the heart of its old city.

Idlib

A northwestern region known for its towns, countryside, olive groves, archaeological heritage, and communities with stories that deserve to be preserved.

MODERN SYRIAN HISTORY

From Assad family rule to revolution, December 8, and Syria’s transition.

Syria’s recent history can be understood through several major periods. This timeline gives visitors the basic sequence before we expand each period with documents, photographs, testimony, and sources.

1970–2000

Hafez al-Assad

Hafez al-Assad took power in 1970 and ruled Syria until his death in 2000, building a highly centralized political and security system.

2000–2011

Bashar al-Assad

Bashar al-Assad succeeded his father in 2000. Expectations of political opening faded as the state remained dominated by centralized rule and powerful security institutions.

2011–2024

Uprising, war, and displacement

Protests calling for political change spread across Syria in 2011 and were met by arrests and violence from security forces. The uprising developed into a devastating war involving Syrian factions and foreign powers, killing hundreds of thousands and displacing millions.

December 8, 2024

The Assad government falls

Opposition forces entered Damascus and Bashar al-Assad fled Syria, ending more than five decades of Assad family rule. For many Syrians who opposed the government, December 8 became a symbol of liberation after years of revolution, imprisonment, displacement, and loss.

2024–Present

Transition and rebuilding

Under President Ahmed al-Sharaa, Syria is rebuilding institutions and international relationships while confronting security, accountability, missing persons, displaced communities, reconstruction, national unity, and the country’s political future.

This section will grow chronologically and with sources so visitors can distinguish documented history, testimony, interpretation, and continuing developments.

STORIES

Every Syrian story is part of Syria’s story.

History is not only written through governments, battles, and major events. It also lives in families, neighborhoods, classrooms, workplaces, journeys, traditions, friendships, losses, and new beginnings.

Stories will become a home for personal memories, eyewitness accounts, family histories, photographs, reflections, and experiences from Syrians inside the country and throughout the diaspora.

By preserving individual voices, we can help future generations understand not only what happened to Syria, but how Syrians lived through it, remembered it, rebuilt their lives, and continued to carry Syria with them wherever they went.

MEDIA WATCH

Claims deserve evidence. Corrections deserve visibility.

Media Watch will examine public reports, headlines, social-media posts, and allegations about Syria where information may be false, misleading, incomplete, unsupported, or disputed.

Our goal is not to reject criticism or label a report false simply because we disagree with it. Each case should clearly identify the original claim, who published it and when, the evidence reviewed, what can be independently verified, what remains disputed or unsupported, and any correction or additional context.

Where appropriate, documented concerns can also be directed through a publisher’s correction process or relevant professional and regulatory channels. The standard is simple: preserve the original record, show the evidence, cite the sources, and let readers see how the conclusion was reached.
